Child support basics

Child support in Minnesota is calculated using a formula set by state law — it's not up for negotiation. Understanding how it works puts you in a better position when you sit down with Jordan.

The Minnesota Child Support Guidelines

Minnesota uses an Income Shares model, which takes into account:

  • Each parent's monthly net disposable income
  • The percentage of time each parent spends with the children
  • Deductions for health insurance, union dues, and other expenses

Courts follow this formula almost always. Deviating from Guideline requires a written agreement from both parents and approval from a judge — it's not common.

What affects the amount?

📅
Timeshare
More overnight time with the lower-earning parent = less support owed to them
💼
Income
Both parents' incomes are factored in — including self-employment and rental income
🏥
Health insurance
The parent paying for the children's health insurance gets a deduction
