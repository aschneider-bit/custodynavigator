Best Custody Schedule Tools (2026)

Finding the right parenting schedule is one of the hardest parts of co-parenting. Once you've figured out the schedule itself, you still need somewhere to track it, share it, and actually stick to it. These nine tools focus specifically on building and managing custody schedules, from dedicated schedule builders to shared calendar apps that keep both households in sync.

1. Custody Navigator

If you're still figuring out what your custody schedule should look like, start here. Custody Navigator lets you browse and customize templates (2-2-3, week-on/week-off, and more), see exactly how each schedule plays out on a calendar, and calculate parenting time percentages before you commit. It's free to use and built specifically for the schedule-building phase. You can export your schedule to Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or your preferred calendar app so you don't have to manually recreate it.

2. CustodyXChange

CustodyXChange combines a visual schedule builder with the ability to generate legally formatted parenting plan documents. You can create detailed custody calendars, calculate parenting time, and produce professional schedules ready for court filings or mediation. It's a paid tool, but useful if you need formal documentation alongside your schedule.

3. Kidtime

Kidtime is a shared custody calendar designed specifically for separated parents. It lets both parents view the custody schedule, track upcoming exchanges, and stay on top of kids' activities. The app sends notifications for schedule changes and upcoming transitions, helping both households stay coordinated without needing to communicate directly about logistics.

4. Togetherforkids

Togetherforkids provides a shared custody calendar where both parents can view the parenting schedule, request changes, and track children's activities. It's designed to keep the focus on the kids' schedule rather than parent-to-parent communication, making it a straightforward option for managing day-to-day custody logistics.

5. Google Calendar

Not a dedicated custody tool, but for parents who communicate well and just need a shared calendar, Google Calendar works fine. Create a shared calendar, color-code each parent's days, and sync it to any device. It's free, works everywhere, and most people already have it. The downside: you'll need to set up the schedule manually since there are no custody-specific templates.

6. Cozi

Cozi is a free family organizer built around a shared calendar, shopping lists, and meal planning. It's not designed for co-parenting specifically, but its color-coded calendar and automatic notifications make it easy for both households to stay on the same page. Each family member gets their own color, so it's simple to see at a glance whose days are whose. A premium tier, Cozi Gold, adds extra features.

7. FamilyWall

FamilyWall combines a shared calendar with a private family feed, to-do lists, and location sharing. Like Cozi, it's a general family organizer rather than a custody-specific tool, but it works well for low-conflict situations where you just need a simple, shared view of the schedule. Free to use with a premium tier available.

8. FamCal

FamCal is a shared family calendar app with color-coded schedules, home screen widgets, and cross-platform sync. Each family member gets their own color and can view the schedule at a glance. It's clean, simple, and focused on being a good shared calendar rather than trying to do everything. Works well for parents who want a lightweight way to keep custody days visible on both phones.

9. TimeTree

TimeTree is a shared calendar app that lets multiple people view and manage the same calendar. You can create separate calendars for different purposes (custody schedule, kids' activities, school events) and chat within individual events. It's free to use, works on all platforms, and supports multiple calendar views including monthly, weekly, and list formats.
